# Shape Nails — Brand Voice Guidelines

For a nail salon website whose voice should read **professional, premium, polite, and welcoming**.

Premium here means *restraint*, not luxury vocabulary. Polite means the guest is never the first
party handed an instruction.

The policies page has the right **register** — plain, confident, respectful of the reader's time.
But its current opening line shows how easily that register tips into brusqueness:

> ✗ "Most of this comes down to two things: arriving on time, and knowing what your service will
> cost before we start."

Two faults, both structural rather than tonal. **"Comes down to" is reductive** — the register of
someone slightly impatient explaining something obvious. And **the first obligation named belongs
to the guest**, which opens the page by framing them as the thing most likely to go wrong.

The corrected form is the north star:

> ✓ "We'll tell you what your service will cost before we begin. All we ask is that you arrive on
> time — everything below is detail."

Same information, same economy, no instruction until the salon has named its own commitment
first. It explains, it doesn't sell, and it assumes an intelligent adult is reading. Every new
piece of copy should sound like it came from that hand.

---

## Voice Constants — We Are / We Are Not

Voice never changes. Tone flexes (see matrix below).

| We Are | We Are Not |
|--------|------------|
| **Composed** — we state things plainly and let the work carry the claim | **Cold or clinical** — composure still sounds like a person who is glad you came |
| **Gracious** — we thank, we welcome, we say "please" and mean it | **Deferential or apologetic** — we don't over-apologize or beg for the booking |
| **Precise** — prices, timings, and conditions are exact and stated up front | **Legalistic** — precision reads as courtesy, never as fine print |
| **Reciprocal** — we name our own commitment before we name what we ask | **Conditional** — we never open by listing what the guest must do |
| **Welcoming** — the door is open; we anticipate the guest's arrival and make the first step easy | **Eager** — a welcome is an open hand, not a raised voice |
| **Warm** — we speak to a guest, not a transaction | **Effusive** — no gushing, no exclamation marks, no flattery |
| **Assured** — we know our craft and say so without qualifying | **Boastful** — we don't claim "the best" or compare ourselves to others |
| **Unhurried** — the writing has room to breathe, like the space itself | **Wordy** — unhurried means measured, not padded |

### The single most useful test

Read the sentence aloud. If it sounds like a salon **advertising itself**, rewrite it.
If it sounds like a well-run salon **telling you how things work**, it's right.

## Tone Flexes by Context

| Context | Formality | Energy | Warmth | Key principle |
|---------|-----------|--------|--------|---------------|
| Home hero / tagline | Medium-High | Low | Medium-High | Say one thing, beautifully. Never explain the tagline. |
| First visit / new guest | Medium | Low-Medium | High | Answer the question they haven't asked yet. |
| Service descriptions | Medium-High | Low | Medium | Describe what happens and what it costs. No adjective stacking. |
| Policies | High | Low | Medium | Explain the *reason*, then the rule. Never scold. |
| Gallery / seasonal | Medium | Low-Medium | Medium-High | Let the images lead; text stays out of the way. |
| Booking / CTA | Medium | Low | High | Invite, don't urge. No scarcity or countdown language. |
| Contact / hours | High | Low | Medium-High | Impeccable information, offered rather than posted. |
| Gift cards | Medium-High | Low | High | A gift is personal — speak to the giver, warmly and briefly. |
| Service recovery | High | Low | High | Acknowledge, state the remedy, thank them. No excuses. |

**Energy is low almost everywhere.** That is deliberate and is most of what separates premium
from ordinary salon copy. Enthusiasm is conveyed through specificity, not punctuation.

## Being Welcoming Without Raising the Volume

Welcoming and premium pull in opposite directions if welcome is treated as *enthusiasm*. A
salon that greets you at full volume feels like it needs the business. The resolution:

> **Welcome is something you remove, not something you add.**

Hospitality in writing is the removal of friction and uncertainty — not the addition of warmth
words. Four mechanics, in order of effectiveness:

**1. Answer the unasked question.** A first-time guest doesn't know how long it takes, what it
costs, whether to bring a reference photo, or what happens if they're five minutes late. Telling
them before they have to ask is the single most welcoming act available on a website. This is
why the policies page already feels hospitable despite being a list of rules.

**2. Offer an option.** Every restriction should leave a door open — the remedy clause in
"Handling Rules and Bad News" is the same principle. "Deposits are non-refundable, **but may be
transferred**" is welcoming. The rule didn't soften; the guest kept a choice.

**3. Extend the invitation explicitly.** Say who is welcome, plainly.
"Walk-ins are welcome, based on availability." "Whether it's your first visit or your fiftieth."
"You're welcome to bring a reference — or none at all."

**4. Use the grammar of hospitality.** These constructions carry welcome at low volume:

| Construction | Example |
|--------------|---------|
| *You're welcome to…* | You're welcome to arrive a few minutes early and settle in. |
| *Whether… or…* | Whether you know exactly what you want or nothing at all. |
| *There's no need to…* | There's no need to book ahead for a classic manicure. |
| *We'll take care of…* | We'll take care of the rest. |
| *Just let us know…* | Just let us know when you arrive. |

**What welcome is not:** "Can't wait to see you!", "We'd LOVE to have you!", "Come on in!!" —
these raise the volume without lowering a single barrier. They perform welcome instead of
providing it.

**The test:** after reading the sentence, does a nervous first-time guest know one more thing
than they did before? If yes, it's welcoming. If it only *sounds* friendly, it isn't.

## Terminology

### Preferred

| Use | Not |
|-----|-----|
| **Shape Nails** (never "The Shape Nails") | The Shape Nails, ShapeNails, Shape Nail |
| **guest** | client, customer, patron |
| **appointment** | booking slot, sesh, session |
| **technician** / **our technicians** | tech, girls, staff members, nail artist (unless describing art specifically) |
| **service** | treatment (reserve "treatment" for spa pedicures) |
| **We recommend booking in advance** | Book now!, Don't miss out, Spots filling fast |
| **Please arrive on time** | Don't be late, Late arrivals will be turned away |
| **We look forward to welcoming you** | Can't wait to see you!, See you soon!! |
| **Walk-ins are welcome, based on availability** | Walk-ins accepted, No appointment necessary |
| **You're welcome to…** | Feel free to…, Don't hesitate to… |
| **Whether it's your first visit or your fiftieth** | New here?, First-timers welcome! |

> **Note:** the site currently mixes *client* and *guest* — `why-choose-us.tsx` and `values.tsx`
> use "client," while the group-bookings policy uses "guests." Standardize on **guest** in
> guest-facing prose. "Client" is acceptable inside policy clauses where it carries a
> contractual sense.

### Prohibited

- **Exclamation marks.** Anywhere in guest-facing prose. This is the fastest downgrade from
  premium to ordinary. Current violations: `gallery/intro.tsx` ("Every Design Tells a Story!",
  "let us bring your story on our canvas!") and `about/values.tsx` ("nails that make a statement!").
- **Hype and superlatives** — "the best," "amazing," "stunning" (as a filler adjective),
  "must-have," "obsessed," "game-changing," "next-level."
- **Casual idiom** — "we've got you covered," "treat yourself," "me time," "pamper sesh,"
  "glow up," "self-care Sunday."
- **Urgency and scarcity** — "limited spots," "hurry," "last chance," "don't miss."
- **Second-person flattery** — "because you deserve it," "you're worth it." It presumes.
- **Reductive framings** — "it all comes down to," "simply put," "all you need to do is,"
  "it's really quite simple." These sound impatient, as though the guest has been slow to
  understand something. Say the thing directly instead of announcing that it is easy.
- **Emoji** in body copy.

### Motif: "story"

The tagline is *Shaping Stories Through Stunning Nails*, and "story" recurs across the gallery,
homepage, and about pages. Keep the motif — it's the brand's one poetic license — but **use it
sparingly**. It currently appears in at least four places, which dilutes it. One deliberate
appearance per page, at most. Never in policies, pricing, or contact copy.

## Typography and Mechanics

The policies page already sets the standard; match it everywhere.

- **Em dashes** (`—`) or en dashes for ranges, not a spaced hyphen. Current copy uses
  `service - it's a story`; it should be `service — it's a story`.
- **Typographic apostrophes** (`'`) and quotes (`"` `"`), not straight ASCII. The policies
  constants already do this correctly (`24 hours' notice`).
- **Prices** always with a currency symbol and no trailing zeros ambiguity: `$50`, `$100`.
- **Times and durations** spelled consistently: `10 minutes`, `24 hours`, `48 hours`, `7 days`.
- **Sentence case** in body prose. Reserve uppercase tracking for display headings, which is
  already the established visual system.
- **Oxford comma.** Yes.
- One space after a period.

## Message Pillars

Every page should sound like it is standing on at least one of these. Not all five.

1. **Craft** — skilled technicians, precision, healthy nails that last. Evidence over adjectives:
   name the technique, the product tier, the wear time.
2. **Calm** — the space is a retreat: clean, modern, unhurried. Convey this through the *pace of
   the sentences*, not by claiming the room is relaxing.
3. **Clarity** — prices, timings, and policies stated before the guest has to ask. This is the
   brand's most distinctive promise and its most premium behavior.
4. **Attention** — services tailored to the individual. Show it with a question we ask, not an
   adjective we apply.
5. **Welcome** — everyone is expected and no one has to guess. Expressed by removing friction
   and uncertainty, never by raising the volume. See "Being Welcoming Without Raising the
   Volume" for the mechanics.

## Handling Rules and Bad News

The policies page gets this right and the pattern should generalize.

**Order of obligation — the rule that governs the others.** When a passage contains both what we
do and what we ask, **ours goes first**. A page that opens with the guest's duty reads as a
notice; the same content with the salon's commitment first reads as an agreement. This is the
difference between a policy page that feels defensive and one that feels professional.

> ✗ "Please arrive on time so we can give you the full service."
> ✓ "We hold your full appointment time for you. Please arrive on time so we can use all of it."

**Structure:** reason → rule → remedy.

> "Please provide at least 24 hours' notice for any cancellation or appointment change.
> Deposits are non-refundable, but may be transferred to another appointment if at least
> 24 hours' notice is provided."

The remedy clause is what keeps a rule polite. When writing any restriction, ask: *have I told
them what they can still do?* If not, the sentence isn't finished.

**Never:** "we reserve the right to," "failure to comply," "will not be tolerated,"
"no exceptions." These are the register of a parking garage, not a salon.

## Worked Examples

**Homepage feature — before (current):**
> Treat yourself to the best - because you deserve nails that make a statement!

**After:**
> Nails shaped, finished, and cared for with the precision the detail deserves.

*Why:* removes the exclamation, the superlative ("the best"), the casual idiom ("treat
yourself"), and the presumptive flattery ("you deserve"). Keeps the confidence, moves the claim
onto the craft.

---

**Gallery intro — before (current):**
> Find your next nail art inspiration and let us bring your story on our canvas!

**After:**
> Bring us a reference, or an idea you haven't quite pictured yet. We'll shape it with you.

*Why:* no exclamation, no strained metaphor. Replaces a slogan with an actual invitation, and
expresses the **Attention** pillar through a concrete offer. It is also more welcoming than the
original despite being quieter — "an idea you haven't quite pictured yet" tells a hesitant guest
they don't need to arrive prepared.

---

**A welcome — before (generic):**
> New to Shape Nails? We'd love to have you! Come on in!

**After:**
> Whether it's your first visit or your fiftieth, you're welcome to book online or simply call.
> Walk-ins are welcome too, based on availability.

*Why:* the original raises the volume without lowering a barrier. The revision names exactly how
to get in the door — three ways — and removes the worry that an appointment is mandatory.
Warmer in effect, quieter on the page.

---

**A rule — before (generic):**
> Late clients will have their service cancelled. No exceptions.

**After:**
> Please arrive on time. Guests arriving more than 10 minutes late may be asked to reschedule,
> or to have the service adjusted to the time remaining.

*Why:* reason-first, softened modal ("may"), and a remedy — the guest keeps an option.

## Open Questions

Unresolved decisions. Apply the recommendation unless directed otherwise.

1. **"Guest" vs "client" sitewide.** *Recommendation:* guest in prose, client in policy clauses.
   Worth a single pass across `why-choose-us.tsx` and `values.tsx` to align.
2. **How far to carry the "story" motif.** *Recommendation:* keep the tagline, thin the
   repetitions to one per page. It currently reads as a theme applied rather than felt.
3. **First person plural throughout?** The site uses "we" consistently and it works.
   *Recommendation:* keep "we"; never switch to "Shape Nails offers…" third person in body copy.
4. **Homepage register vs policies register.** The policies page is meaningfully more premium
   than the homepage right now. *Recommendation:* bring the homepage up to the policies
   standard, not the reverse.
